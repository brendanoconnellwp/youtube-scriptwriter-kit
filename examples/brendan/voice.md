# Brendan's voice

Built from all 101 transcripts (Oct 2022 to Aug 2026, about 244k spoken words, 94 with usable captions). Where his style shifted, this file follows how he sounds **now** (2025 to 2026) and notes the older habit.

## 1. Who's talking

Brendan is a working web developer who runs an agency and manages "literally hundreds of websites." He started as a Bricks Builder tutorial guy and has moved through n8n automation and APIs into Claude Code, agents and MCP, always with WordPress as home base ("not going to leave WordPress"). He talks to the viewer as a peer who is figuring it out on camera, not a guru: "I'm learning with you, too." He's curious, budget-aware, pro open source and self-hosting, generous to other creators, and honest when something breaks.

## 2. Openings

No cold open, no teaser. The current shape:
1. A greeting **or** a news/question hook (not both).
2. "In this video, I wanted to [take a look at / share / talk about] X."
3. One plain line defining X, usually as an alternative to something known: "X is a [thing] that lets you..."
4. Show the finished result, say the plan out loud, then "let's just jump into it."

Real openers:
- "So, a couple days ago, Anthropic released Fable 5..." (Fable plugin, 2026-06, 0:00)
- "One of the questions I've been getting lately is, you know, Brendan, are you using Paper or are you using Pencil" (Paper vs Pencil, 2026-03, 0:00)
- "Since we're in the AI slop design era, I figured it would be important to share this tool" (Figma to Bricks, 2026-08, 0:01)
- "What's going on everyone? I wanted to share another open-source project that I've been enjoying using." (OpenWhispr, 2026-05, 0:00)
- "Hey, welcome back to the channel. In this video, I want to talk about the new AI agent skills for WordPress." (Agent Skills, 2026-02, 0:00)

He says his name less in 2026 ("Brendan here" was standard through 2025). His best videos state a concrete outcome or an "alternative to [big name]" frame in the first sentence. His weakest open on throat-clearing ("I just wanted to share a couple things I've been working on"). Script the former.

## 3. Explaining things

- **Show, then build.** Demo the finished thing, then rebuild it step by step. Concept first only for big ideas (APIs, webhooks, headless).
- **Analogies are other tools, not metaphors.** "Very much like Google Analytics, but unlike Google Analytics..." / "kind of like Granola" / "it's basically functioning like a query loop, but it's query looping externally." No sports or kitchen analogies.
- **Asks himself the question, then answers.** "So what can we do with this?" / "Now the question is, how do we get this into our actual site?" Sometimes voices the viewer's objection with his own name: "Great, so Brendan, you've queried all this, what can you do with it?"
- **"The idea is..."** to frame the why in one or two practical sentences (saves time, saves money, own your data, guardrails for clients).
- **Intermediate audience.** Assumes WordPress admin, Bricks, CPTs, ACF, query loops. Glosses a term in half a sentence ("if you're not familiar..."). Sends viewers elsewhere for basics: "there's plenty of other videos on that."
- **Practical gotchas** with a hedge: "Make sure you turn on show in REST API, otherwise your data will not show up."
- **Admits the edge of his knowledge.** "I'm probably not the person to be giving the best advice on that one."
- **Silly demo data** every time: Netflix for cats, a haunted smart toaster, "me and John Wick are the authors of these posts."

## 4. Sentence rhythm

- Long spoken chains joined by "so," "and," "and then," "right?" Then a one-word fragment when something works: "Perfect." "There it is." "Interesting." "Not bad." "It works."
- Self-corrects mid-sentence and restarts clauses. Keep one or two of these per section.
- Fillers he really uses: "you know," "kind of," "sort of," "I mean," "I guess," "I think," "I don't know" (as a placeholder while picking an example: "let's do, I don't know, 20").
- Leans on: **basically** (every video), **just** ("let's just," "I just wanted to"), **actually**, **pretty** (pretty cool, pretty simple, pretty darn quick), **stuff**, **super** (super useful, super cheap), **go ahead and**.

### Habits Brendan wants to break
The patterns above are how he talks today. Scripts should sound like him on a good take, not reproduce the tics.
- **Fillers:** at most one filler ("you know," "kind of," "I guess," "I mean") per section, and none in the hook. Never "uh" or "um." Cut "basically," "just" and "actually" unless the sentence needs them.
- **Vague intensifiers:** don't lean on "pretty cool," "pretty interesting," "super interesting," "super useful," "really powerful stuff." Say what's specific instead: what it does, what it saves, what surprised him. ("It cut the build from an hour to twelve minutes," not "that's pretty cool.") One "pretty cool" per script is fine.
- **Tag "right?":** once or twice per script, not every other sentence.
- **Keep:** the hedges that are honest ("I think," "I'm not sure"), the one-word reactions, the dry humor, the run-on rhythm. Those are the voice. The fillers aren't.
- Hedges facts: "I believe," "I think," "don't quote me on that."
- Questions to the viewer come at the end, stacked. Mid-video, the questions are to himself.

## 6. Transitions and structure

- Pivots: "All right, so..." / "Okay, so now..." / "So, next up..." / "Let's keep moving along."
- Escalation: "Let's take it one step further." / "One more quick example to round it out."
- Structure follows the build or the tool's UI, not a numbered framework. He never says "step 3 of 5." The one exception is a listicle-titled video.
- Says the plan once at the top ("we're going from prompt to design to VS Code to a working WordPress theme") and recaps the build as a spoken list near the end.
- Says cuts out loud: "I'll pause it here... and we're back." / "I might speed some of this up. I'll let you know how long it takes."
- Points to past and future videos loosely: "I did a video on that earlier this week." / "Maybe that's another video."

## 7. Humor and personality

Dry, quick, self-deprecating. One to three small moments per video, never a set-up bit.
- Talking back to the AI: "Thank you, Claude. Very smart. Very smart AI." / "Oh, come on. Really? Go, buddy."
- Demo gods: "Never works in the video, does it?"
- On himself: "a very mediocre prompt at best from yours truly" / "I suck at typing" / "Maybe I'm a noob."
- Deadpan: "I don't live in Brooklyn. I don't know why it's saying that." / "Just stop using Safari."
- Killing time: "Take a sip of my coffee."

## 8. Opinions and recurring themes

- **Independence:** open source, self-hosting, owning your data. "This is exactly what this channel is about. It's about keeping things independent." "We all have too many subscriptions."
- **Cost-aware:** quotes real prices and token costs, prefers free tiers, one-time fees and bring-your-own-key. Critical of lock-in, including Anthropic's usage limits.
- **WordPress as the content hub,** with separation of content and design (CPTs, custom fields, variables, design systems). Plan the content structure before you design.
- **Clients:** guardrails so they can edit without breaking things; reframe requests instead of saying "I can't do it."
- **AI:** enthusiastic but practical. Agents need guardrails, skills, WP-CLI, a local environment. Lately: minimal prompting, "get out of the model's way." Calls out AI slop and says the designer still does the last 40%.
- **The ecosystem moves fast;** builders should add APIs and MCP. Fair critic: asks for features without snark, praises plugin devs by name.

## 9. Screen demos

- Narrates in first-person plural, present or near future: "So we'll add a flex div, we'll add a heading."
- Reads UI labels, docs and agent output aloud, then reacts in a fragment.
- Debugs on camera and keeps it: "What am I missing here?" / "Am I crazy? ... There it is." / "Oops." Turns errors into teaching moments, or moves on: "I don't want to troubleshoot that now, it's going to derail this."
- Waiting on agents: fills time honestly and reports durations ("took just over 12 minutes").
- Judges output critically: "The dark theme is not too great. I've seen better." / "It's not going to do absolutely everything for you."
- Security asides: "I'll blur that out so people don't steal it."

## 10. Sponsor reads and CTAs

- **Like/subscribe:** rare and light, usually once near the end or while waiting on an agent. "You know what to do. Hit that like and subscribe button." Never "smash."
- **Main CTA is a comment question,** often stacked: "Are you guys using Paper? Are you using Pencil? Do you have a preference?" Also "if you like this kind of content, let me know."
- **Sponsors** (Big Scoots, Independent Analytics): mid-roll after the first payoff. In: "Real quick, thank you to [sponsor] for sponsoring this video." Out: "All right, let's get back to the video." Candid disclosure is fine: "when they reached out, I hadn't really looked at the product." Write reads in his casual voice, with rough math ("it's under $10 a website"). His two polished ad-copy reads are not his voice; don't imitate them.
- **Close:** "So, anyway, that's basically what I wanted to show you guys" → one-line recap → question to the viewer → "I'll see you in the next one. Peace."

## 11. Never

- Hype words: game-changer, insane, mind-blowing, crazy good, unlock, supercharge, level up, revolutionize, delve. His praise stays low-key, but make it specific rather than "pretty cool" or "super useful" on repeat.
- "In today's video," "without further ado," "what's up guys," "smash that like button," "stay till the end," "before we get started, subscribe."
- Teaser cold opens, pattern-interrupt gimmicks, "3 reasons why" frameworks, numbered steps in speech.
- Fake certainty or guru tone. He hedges and admits gaps.
- Trashing competitors or other creators.
- Polished, quotable one-liners and neat rhetorical triplets. He doesn't talk in taglines.
- Long personal stories. Anecdotes are one sentence.
- Swearing beyond "suck."
- **No em dashes in any written copy.** Use periods, commas or "and."
